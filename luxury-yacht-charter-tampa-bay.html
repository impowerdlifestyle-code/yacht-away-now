Why We Leave From the St. Pete Side

Tampa Bay is a big, shallow body of water and where you start decides what you see. Our dock is at Maximo Marina, 38th Way S in south St. Petersburg, at the mouth of the bay. From here the Sunshine Skyway is 35 minutes away, the Shell Key sandbar 25 minutes, and Egmont Key under an hour. A charter leaving from the Tampa waterfront spends its first hour and its last hour motoring down the shipping channel before it reaches any of that.

For guests coming from Tampa, Brandon or Wesley Chapel, the drive across the Howard Frankland or the Gandy is 30 to 40 minutes, roughly what you would spend in the channel anyway, and you spend it in air conditioning rather than watching the bridge get bigger. Guests from Clearwater, Sarasota and the beaches are closer still.

Routes

Four Ways to See the Bay

The captain builds the route around your hours and the tide. These are the four directions a Tampa Bay luxury charter can point from our dock.

North to the Tampa Skyline

The longest run on the bay, and the one that needs an eight-hour charter to do properly. You cross under the Skyway approach, run up the middle of the bay past MacDill Air Force Base, and arrive off Bayshore Boulevard with downtown Tampa, the Riverwalk towers and Davis Islands laid out in front of you. Anchor for lunch off the Davis Islands yacht basin, then cruise home with the afternoon sun behind you.

Under the Sunshine Skyway

Four miles of cable-stayed bridge, 430 feet of tower, and a view of it from underneath that no car on it ever gets. This is the backbone of the Golden Hour Sunset Cruise: out past Fort De Soto, under the span, and anchored in the Gulf with the whole bridge silhouetted against the sunset. It fits inside four hours with time to spare.

Egmont Key

An island at the mouth of the bay that you can only reach by boat: the 1858 lighthouse, the brick ruins of Fort Dade, gopher tortoises on the paths and snorkeling over the submerged gun batteries on the Gulf side. The five-hour Shell Key and Egmont Key Island Day pairs it with a sandbar swim on the way out.

Shell Key

A 1,800-acre preserve of sandbar and bird rookery just south of Pass-a-Grille, and the closest stop to our dock. The water on the Gulf side goes from waist-deep to shin-deep across a hundred yards of white sand, which makes it the swim stop for families, bachelorette parties and anyone who wants a photo standing in the middle of the bay.

The Fleet

Two Yachts, One Dock

The 52ft Marquis Flybridge is the day boat: three decks, a 1,200 square foot climate-controlled cabin, more than 800 square feet of open deck, two cabins, premium audio on every level, and a cruising speed above 30 knots that turns the far side of the bay into a reachable lunch stop. All-Inclusive

The Price Is the Price

The hourly rate covers the yacht, Captain Josh Wilson, crew, fuel, ice and cooler space, life jackets for every guest, and the freedom to change the route on the water. Food and drink are BYOB with no corkage; glass is decanted into plastic at the dock. The only thing outside the rate is a captain gratuity of 10 to 15 percent of the base, which is at your discretion.

A 50 percent deposit holds your date and the balance is due seven days before departure. If a named storm is forecast within 72 hours of your charter you choose any open future date at no cost, and the booking stays valid for 12 months. Ordinary weather is managed on the day by shifting departure times.

Most Tampa Bay luxury charters book in one of three shapes: four hours ($2,000 weekday, $2,400 weekend) for a sunset or a downtown cruise; five hours ($2,500 weekday, $3,000 weekend) for the islands; or eight hours ($4,000 weekday, $4,800 weekend) for the skyline run or a full Gulf day. Tampa Bay Luxury Charter FAQ

Common Questions

How much does a luxury yacht charter on Tampa Bay cost?

With Yacht Away Now it is $500 an hour Monday through Thursday and $600 an hour Friday through Sunday, four-hour minimum, all-inclusive. Captain, crew, fuel and ice are in the rate. Sly Fox, our overnight yacht, is $650 and $750 an hour. Captain gratuity of 10 to 15 percent is at your discretion.

Do you pick up in Tampa?

No. Both yachts depart from Maximo Marina at 38th Way S in St. Petersburg, at the mouth of the bay, where the Skyway, Shell Key and Egmont Key are all within an hour. From Tampa the drive is 30 to 40 minutes over the Howard Frankland or Gandy bridges, about the time a boat leaving Tampa would spend in the shipping channel.

Can we cruise to the Tampa skyline?

Yes, on an eight-hour charter. The run up the bay to Bayshore Boulevard and Davis Islands is the longest route we offer and needs the full day to include a stop at anchor. Four and five hour charters stay in the southern bay and the Gulf: the Skyway, Fort De Soto, Shell Key and Egmont Key.

What is included in an all-inclusive charter?

The yacht, USCG Master Josh Wilson and crew, fuel, ice and cooler space, life jackets for every guest, premium audio, Starlink WiFi and a route planned around your group. Food and drink are BYOB with no corkage; glass is moved to plastic at the dock. Nothing else is added to the hourly rate.

How do I book, and what if a storm comes?

Request a date by phone or the booking form; we confirm within 24 hours. A 50 percent deposit holds the date and the balance is due seven days before departure. If a named storm is forecast within 72 hours of your charter you choose any open future date at no cost, valid for 12 months.

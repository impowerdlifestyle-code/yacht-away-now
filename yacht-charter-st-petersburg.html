The St. Pete Waterfront

A Skyline Best Seen From the Water

Few American cities are this beautiful from a yacht's deck. Here is what your guests will see as we cruise the bay.

St. Pete Pier & Pier District

Our most-requested route opens with the new St. Pete Pier — the 26-acre waterfront destination that reopened in 2020 with the soaring Pier Head, the Pelican fishing deck, and Spa Beach. From the water, the entire Pier complex is dramatic at sunset: amber light hitting the Pier Head's white spine, kids splashing at Spa Beach, and the Doc Ford's Rum Bar patio glowing on the south side. We slow-cruise the pier perimeter at golden hour — every guest gets the photo.

The Vinoy Resort & Demens Landing

Just north of the Pier, the iconic pink-stucco Vinoy Resort & Golf Club has anchored North Yacht Basin since 1925. We pass the Vinoy's seawall on most northbound routes — the Mediterranean Revival towers are unmistakable from the water. Adjacent Demens Landing Park juts out into the bay between the Vinoy and the Pier, a green peninsula lined with palm trees and small-craft moorings. Couples celebrating anniversaries often request this exact stretch for sunset photos.

Mahaffey Theater, Dali Museum & the Downtown Waterfront

South of the Pier, the bay opens onto the Mahaffey Theater's concert hall, the geometric facade of the Salvador Dali Museum, and the cluster of downtown high-rises. On weekend evenings you'll see the Mahaffey marquee lit up from offshore — a postcard view that no street-level vantage point can match. The Dali, with its glass enigma jutting from a hurricane-rated concrete shell, looks particularly cinematic in the late-afternoon light.

Tropicana Field Sightline & Old Northeast

Look inland from Tampa Bay on a clear day and you'll see the white-domed roof of Tropicana Field over the downtown skyline — the Rays' home stadium punctuating an otherwise low-rise city. Further north, the historic Old Northeast neighborhood's tree-lined waterfront and Snell Isle's Mediterranean estates create the cleanest residential skyline view in Tampa Bay. Many of our guests request a slow loop along the Snell Isle seawall as part of a longer northbound charter.

Common Questions

How much does it cost to rent a yacht in St. Petersburg?

Yacht rentals in St. Petersburg with Yacht Away Now start at $500/hour on weekdays (4-hour minimum, all-inclusive). Weekend cruises are $600/hour, with set 4-hour ($2,400) and 5-hour ($3,000) packages. Captain, crew, and fuel are always included — there are no hidden fees.

Can you rent a yacht for a day in St. Pete?

Yes. You can rent our 52ft Marquis Flybridge for a full day in St. Petersburg — a popular choice for island-hopping to Shell Key and Egmont Key, sandbar days, and celebrations. Day rentals include a USCG-licensed captain and crew, so no boating license is required.

Where do your St. Petersburg yacht rentals depart from?

Every charter departs directly from our private marina at 38th Way S in St. Petersburg, FL 33711 — not Tampa or Clearwater. That means zero shuttle time and the most direct access to the St. Pete Pier, the Vinoy, Shell Key, and the Skyway. Shell Key is about 25 minutes from the dock.

Do you need a license to rent a yacht in St. Petersburg?

No. Because every Yacht Away Now rental is fully captained, you do not need a boating license or any experience. Our USCG-licensed captain handles the vessel while you and up to 12 guests relax and enjoy the water.

Our 52ft Marquis Flybridge is USCG-inspected for up to 13 guests, with three decks and a 1,200 sq ft climate-controlled cabin — comfortable for everything from an intimate sunset cruise to a 13-person celebration.

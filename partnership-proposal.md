## 1. Executive Summary

This proposal outlines a two-tier business model for Yacht Away Now:

- **Tier 1 — Own Fleet:** Josh provides 100% of vessel capital; Ciaran provides 100% of the business build-out (platform, AI, SEO, marketing). 60/40 profit split (Josh/Ciaran).
- **Tier 2 — Brokerage Platform:** Other yacht owners list vessels on yachtawaynow.com; we facilitate bookings and take a 30-45% commission

The goal is to build a scalable charter platform in the Tampa Bay market that generates income from both owned assets and brokerage fees with minimal marginal cost per listing.

### 2.5 Additional Vessel Acquisition Models (For Scaling)

#### Model A — Revenue-Funded Acquisition

- Use brokerage + charter profits to fund the down payment on additional vessels
- The LLC buys the boat — cleaner for taxes and liability
- No additional personal capital required from either partner
- **Best for:** Vessel #2 and beyond once cash flow is proven

#### Model B — Lease-to-Charter (Asset-Light)

- Lease or rent a vessel on a monthly/seasonal basis instead of purchasing
- Charter it out at a markup
- Lower upfront capital ($3K-$8K/month lease vs. $30K+ down payment)
- Can upgrade or swap vessels seasonally based on demand
- **Best for:** Testing a new vessel type or market segment with minimal risk

#### Model C — Fractional / Syndicate Ownership

- Bring in 2-4 outside investors who each own a percentage of a vessel
- Ciaran & Josh manage operations via the LLC and take a management fee (10-15% of gross) plus profit share
- Investors get passive income from charter revenue
- **Best for:** Scaling to a 3-5 vessel fleet without Josh over-leveraging on capital

#### Model D — Vessel Partnership with Existing Owners

- Partner with someone who already owns a yacht but isn't using it enough
- We market, book, and manage charters on their vessel
- Split: 30-45% to us (see Tier 2 commission structure), 55-70% to owner
- Owner covers insurance, maintenance, and slip fees since it's their asset
- **Best for:** Zero capital required, immediate inventory, easy to pitch to underutilized yacht owners

### 2.6 Recommended Growth Path

**Phase 1 (Months 1-6):** Josh funds first vessel. Ciaran launches platform, AI systems, and SEO. Begin taking bookings on own vessel + recruit first 5 brokerage owners (Model D). Prove the model.

**Phase 2 (Months 7-12):** Use proven booking data and cash flow to add brokerage listings aggressively. Evaluate adding a second vessel via Model A (revenue-funded) or Model B (lease).

**Phase 3 (Year 2+):** Scale fleet via Model C (syndicate investors) or Model A purchases funded by cash flow. Brokerage platform becomes the primary growth engine with 30+ listed vessels.

## 3. Tier 2: Brokerage Platform Model

### 3.1 Concept

Yacht Away Now operates as a booking platform where third-party yacht owners list their vessels. We handle marketing, booking management, payment processing, and customer service. Owners handle the actual charter operations.

### 3.2 Commission Structure Options

| Model | Our Commission | Owner Gets | Best For |
|-------|---------------|-----------|---------|
| **A — Flat 30%** | 30% of booking | 70% | Standard — owner provides vessel + crew, we do everything else |
| **B — Full-Service 40-45%** | 40-45% of booking | 55-60% | We handle marketing, booking, customer service, payment, AND crew coordination |
| **C — Tiered by Volume** | 40% first $5K/mo, 30% above | Scales with volume | Rewards high-performing vessels, keeps owners motivated |
| **D — Subscription + Commission** | $199/mo + 30% | 70% minus sub fee | Premium owners wanting guaranteed platform commitment |

**Why 30-45% is justified:**

- We're not just a listing site — we're a full-service booking and marketing engine
- AI-powered instant inquiry responses convert leads that owners would otherwise lose
- Professional SEO drives organic traffic owners can't generate themselves
- Payment processing, customer service, and review management are all handled
- Charter management companies typically charge 30-50%, so this is market rate
- GetMyBoat charges ~20% but provides minimal marketing — our value-add justifies the premium

**Recommended:** Start at 35% flat for standard listings, 45% for full-service (where we also coordinate crew scheduling and handle all customer communication end-to-end). Owners who bring their own bookings through the platform pay 30%.

### 3.3 Value Proposition to Yacht Owners

- **Full booking pipeline** — We drive leads, respond instantly with AI, close bookings, and handle payment
- **Local SEO dominance** — Tampa Bay focused, not buried in a national marketplace with 10,000 listings
- **AI-powered inquiry response** — Instant draft replies to booking inquiries (already built) — owners lose leads when they take 6+ hours to respond; we respond in minutes
- **Professional listing pages** — High-quality photos, optimized descriptions, review integration
- **Review management** — Automated Google review responses building social proof
- **Zero effort bookings** — Owner just shows up, does the charter, gets paid
- **No exclusivity required** — Owners can list on multiple platforms simultaneously
- **Transparent reporting** — Monthly dashboard showing bookings, revenue, and performance

## 11. Alternative Structure — Shared Overhead & Equity Buy-Up

As an alternative (or evolution) of the 60/40 split, both partners can share all overhead costs equally. Ciaran would use his profit share to cover his portion of overhead expenses AND contribute toward paying off the yacht. In return, Ciaran's ownership and equity in the business increases proportionally as he pays down the vessel.

**How it works:**

- All overhead costs (slip fees, insurance, maintenance, fuel, crew, etc.) are split between both partners
- Ciaran directs his profit distributions toward covering his share of overhead + paying down the yacht principal
- As Ciaran pays down more of the yacht, his equity stake in the LLC increases accordingly
- Josh's initial 60% ownership gradually adjusts as Ciaran buys in with real dollars

**Example Equity Trajectory:**

| Milestone | Josh Equity | Ciaran Equity | Trigger |
|-----------|------------|---------------|---------|
| Launch | 60% | 40% | Josh funds vessel, Ciaran builds platform |
| Ciaran covers 25% of yacht cost | 55% | 45% | Profit reinvestment toward vessel payoff |
| Ciaran covers 50% of yacht cost | 50% | 50% | Equal financial + build contribution — max equity |

**Why this works for both partners:**

- **For Josh:** Reduces his financial burden over time as Ciaran shares overhead and pays down the yacht. Less personal capital at risk as the business matures.
- **For Ciaran:** Creates a path to equal ownership (50/50) through reinvestment rather than upfront capital. Aligns incentives — the harder Ciaran works to drive bookings, the faster he earns equity.
- **For the business:** Both partners become increasingly invested (financially and operationally), reducing the risk of one partner disengaging.

**Key terms to define:**
- [ ] How is yacht payoff value tracked? (Monthly statements, shared ledger)
- [ ] At what intervals does equity adjust? (Quarterly, annually)
- [ ] Is there a cap on equity shift? (e.g., Ciaran maxes at 50%?)
- [ ] Does this apply to future vessels acquired by the LLC?

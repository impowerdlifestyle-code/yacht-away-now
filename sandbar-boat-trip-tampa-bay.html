The Sandbar Experience

Tampa Bay's Best Sandbars

Tampa Bay is famous for its shallow, crystal-clear sandbars — natural strips of white sand that emerge at low tide, creating the perfect spot to wade, swim, and party in knee-deep turquoise water. A sandbar boat trip is the quintessential Florida Gulf Coast experience, and there is no better way to do it than aboard a private luxury yacht.

Our 52ft Marquis Flybridge anchors directly at the sandbar, and you step off the swim platform into warm, shallow water. Bring a cooler, set up on the sandbar, blast your playlist through our premium Bluetooth stereo, and spend the day living the Florida dream. Most sandbar trips combine 2-3 hours at the bar with cruising, dolphin spotting, and a sunset return.

Fort De Soto Sandbar is the most popular — a massive shallow flat near the Skyway Bridge with stunning views and calm water. Shell Key Sandbar offers a more secluded experience with pristine shelling on the adjacent island. Egmont Key Sandbar combines history (Civil War-era fort ruins) with incredible snorkeling. Beer Can Island (Longboat Key) is the party sandbar — a social scene where boaters raft up on weekends.

Unlike renting a small boat and navigating unfamiliar waters yourself, a private yacht sandbar trip includes a USCG-licensed captain who handles everything — anchoring, tide timing, and route planning. You just show up with your food and drinks (BYOB) and enjoy the day.

Fort De Soto Sandbar

The crown jewel of Tampa Bay sandbars. Massive shallow flat near the Skyway Bridge with panoramic views, calm water, and easy anchoring for our yacht.

Shell Key Sandbar

More secluded and pristine. Combine sandbar lounging with world-class shelling on the adjacent undeveloped barrier island. Dolphins frequently cruise through.

Egmont Key Sandbar

History meets paradise. Explore Civil War-era fort ruins, then snorkel the crystal-clear waters around this remote island at the mouth of Tampa Bay.

Common Questions

Sandbar Trip FAQ

Which Tampa Bay sandbar is the best?

Fort De Soto sandbar is the most popular — massive, calm, and near the Skyway Bridge. Shell Key sandbar is more secluded with great shelling. Egmont Key combines snorkeling with Civil War history. Your captain will recommend the best option based on tides and weather.

Does the tide affect sandbar trips?

Yes — sandbars are most visible and accessible at low tide when shallow areas emerge. Our captain monitors tide charts and plans the route to arrive at the sandbar during optimal conditions.

Can you walk on the sandbar?

Absolutely. At low tide, the water at Fort De Soto and Shell Key sandbars is often knee-deep or shallower. You can wade, walk, lounge, and set up on the sand. The yacht anchors nearby and you access the sandbar from the swim platform.

Are sandbar trips safe for kids?

Yes — the shallow, calm water at sandbars is ideal for children. Life jackets are provided for all guests. Kids 6 and under must wear them at all times. Many families choose sandbar trips specifically because of the safe, shallow swimming conditions.

What should I bring to the sandbar?

Bring towels, sunscreen, a cooler with food and drinks (BYOB — no glass), swimsuits, and water shoes. We provide ice and cooler space on the yacht. A waterproof phone case is recommended.

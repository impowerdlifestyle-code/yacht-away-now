The Dolphin Experience

Private Dolphin Watching

Tampa Bay is home to one of the largest populations of Atlantic bottlenose dolphins in the United States. With over 600 resident dolphins in the bay, sightings are nearly guaranteed on every charter — especially during morning and late afternoon cruises when dolphins are most active near the surface.

Unlike crowded commercial dolphin tour boats that pack 50+ passengers onto a single vessel, a Yacht Away Now dolphin cruise is a completely private experience. Your group of up to 13 guests has the entire 52ft Marquis Flybridge to yourselves — three decks of viewing space, a professional USCG-licensed captain who knows the dolphins' favorite spots, and the freedom to linger at every sighting.

Our captain regularly encounters dolphin pods near Egmont Key, the Skyway Bridge pilings, Pass-a-Grille channel, Shell Key, and the Fort De Soto shoreline. These are not random encounters — Tampa Bay's dolphins follow predictable feeding patterns, and our crew knows exactly where to find them based on tide, time of day, and season. Morning charters (before 11am) and late afternoon runs (after 3pm) offer the highest concentration of sightings.

Where & When

Finding Tampa Bay's Resident Dolphins

Our captain knows the bay — here's where the dolphins are and the best time to find them.

A year-round resident population

Tampa Bay is home to a year-round resident community of bottlenose dolphins — these aren't seasonal visitors, they live and feed here every month of the year. That's why a private dolphin cruise from St. Petersburg has such a high success rate: our captain runs this water constantly and knows the pods' habits. You're not chasing a rumor; you're going where the dolphins reliably are.

The best sighting zones

Dolphins concentrate where the bait does: the Skyway Bridge pilings, the edges of the shipping channel, and the grass flats around Shell Key and Egmont Key. We work these areas on a typical cruise, and dolphins frequently come to bow-ride — surfing the pressure wave off the front of the boat just feet from the deck. In the cooler winter months, keep an eye out for manatees too, which gather near warm-water outflows around the bay.

Morning and late afternoon are prime

Dolphins are most active during their morning and late-afternoon feeding windows, when calmer light also makes them easiest to spot and photograph. A late-afternoon cruise doubles as a sunset cruise — dolphins working the bait, then the sky lighting up over the Gulf on the way home. Because the charter is private, we can linger whenever you find a pod instead of moving you along on a schedule.

Common Questions

Dolphin Cruise FAQ

Are dolphin sightings guaranteed?

While we cannot guarantee wildlife encounters, Tampa Bay has over 600 resident bottlenose dolphins and our captain knows their feeding routes. Most charters see multiple dolphin pods, especially during morning and late afternoon cruises.

What is the best time of day for a dolphin cruise?

Early morning (before 11am) and late afternoon (after 3pm) offer the highest concentration of dolphin activity. Dolphins feed near the surface during these times, making them easier to spot from the yacht.

What is the best season for dolphin watching in Tampa Bay?

Dolphins are present year-round in Tampa Bay. Spring (March-May) and fall (September-November) tend to have the calmest waters and most active dolphin behavior. Summer is also excellent but can be warmer.

Can children come on a dolphin cruise?

Yes! Dolphin cruises are family-friendly and children of all ages are welcome. Kids 6 and under must wear a life jacket at all times. The yacht accommodates up to 13 guests total.

What should I bring on a dolphin cruise?

Bring sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat, and a camera with good zoom. All charters are BYOB — bring your own food, drinks, and snacks. No glass containers allowed aboard.

Your First Yacht Charter: What to Expect, Step by Step

From booking to the ride home — exactly how a private charter day unfolds, so you can board like a regular.

July 2026 6 min read
JW
Josh Wilson
Founder & USCG Master 100-Ton Captain

Booking your first private yacht charter comes with a surprising number of small questions. Where do I park? Do I board early? Am I supposed to tip? Who decides where we go? After hundreds of charters and 137 five-star Google reviews, we've heard them all — so here is the entire day, start to finish, exactly as it happens.

Step 1: Booking

Pick your date, group size, and hours (4-hour minimum), and reserve with a 50% deposit. Weekday charters run $500/hour and weekends $600/hour, all-inclusive. You'll get a confirmation with the departure location, arrival time, and a direct line to your captain. In the days before the trip, we'll check in about your plans — celebration, sandbar day, sunset cruise — so the crew can prep.

Step 2: Arrival and Boarding

Plan to arrive about 15 minutes before departure at our private St. Petersburg marina (the exact address is in your confirmation). There's parking steps from the slip. The crew will help carry coolers and bags aboard — remember, charters are BYOB, no glass — and shoes come off at the boarding step to protect the decks. Barefoot is the dress code.

Step 3: The Safety Briefing

Before lines come off the dock, your captain gives a short briefing: where life jackets live (all sizes aboard, including kids'), how to move between decks while underway, and how the swim platform works. It takes five minutes and it's the only "formal" part of the day.

Step 4: Choosing the Route

This is the part first-timers don't expect: you have a real say in the itinerary. The captain will lay out the day's options based on wind, tide, and your group's energy — anchor at a quiet sandbar, run out to Shell Key or Egmont Key, cruise under the Skyway Bridge, or chase the golden hour along Pass-a-Grille. Want more swimming and less cruising? Say so. It's your charter.

Step 5: The Day Itself

Once underway, there's genuinely nothing you're responsible for. The crew handles navigation, anchoring, music requests on the premium stereo, and drink runs to the coolers. Dolphins usually find the boat before you find them. Most 4-hour charters settle into a rhythm: a cruise out, a long swim stop, food and drinks on the flybridge, and a slow ride home as the light turns gold.

Step 6: Heading Back

Your captain times the return so you're back at the dock at the end of your booked window — never rushed, never short-changed. Gather your things, and if the crew made your day, a 10–15% tip handed directly to them is the customary thank-you. Then start the group chat about the next one. Ready for your first charter? What's Included, So You Don't Double-Pack

The all-inclusive rate covers your USCG-licensed captain, crew, and fuel. The price you see is the price you pay. The boat also carries the gear first-timers most often buy unnecessarily: coolers and ice are provided, life jackets in every size are aboard, the premium Bluetooth stereo means no speaker to lug, and Starlink WiFi keeps phones working offshore. The full galley has a refrigerator for anything perishable. What you actually bring is short: food and drinks (charters are BYOB with no corkage fee), a towel per person, reef-safe sunscreen, and a light layer for the evening. The complete list is in our packing guide.

Five Mistakes First-Timers Make

Bringing glass. It's the one item we turn away at the dock. Decant into cans, plastic, or koozies before you leave the house and boarding takes two minutes instead of ten.

Overpacking. Every first-time group shows up with a folding chair or a bag of ice we already have. If it's related to running the boat or keeping drinks cold, it's handled.

Skipping the seasickness plan. Tampa Bay is protected water and most guests never feel a thing, but Dramamine or Bonine only works taken 30 to 60 minutes before departure. Have it and not need it.

Wrong shoes. Black-soled shoes mark the decks and heels don't belong on a boat. Soft soles or barefoot, and water shoes if you plan to wade onto a sandbar.

No layer for the ride home. The breeze picks up after sundown and the flybridge feels cooler than the dock did at noon. A hoodie in the bag costs nothing and saves the last hour.

Picking Your Time Slot

Our typical windows are 10am to 2pm, 2:30 to 6:30pm, and sunset. For a first charter, match the slot to the season. In summer, book the morning or the sunset and let the mid-afternoon storm window have the middle of the day; the evening light after a storm clears is the best of the year. In winter, sunset lands before 6pm, so an afternoon charter catches full golden hour. April, May, and October are the statistical sweet spots all day long. The Policies in Plain English

A 50% deposit locks your date, and the balance is due 7 days before departure. Need to move the date? We reschedule flexibly within availability. If the National Hurricane Center or National Weather Service issues an advisory for our waters within 72 hours of your charter, you pick any open future date at no extra cost, and the booking stays valid for 12 months. The person booking must be 21 or older, and guests under 18 need a parent or guardian aboard. That's the whole fine print.

Your First Yacht Charter — FAQ

Do I need a boating license to charter a yacht?

No. Every Yacht Away Now charter is fully crewed — a USCG-licensed captain runs the yacht while you relax. No boating license, experience, or knowledge is required from anyone in your group.

Do you tip the captain and crew on a yacht charter?

Tipping is customary and always appreciated. Most guests tip 10–15% of the charter rate, given directly to the crew at the end of the trip — similar to what you'd tip for excellent restaurant service.

Can I choose where the yacht goes?

Yes, within safe conditions. Before departure your captain will walk through options — sandbars, Shell Key, Egmont Key, the Skyway, a sunset loop — and build the route around what your group wants. Weather and tides get the final say, and your captain adjusts to keep the day smooth.

What happens if the weather is bad on my charter day?

Your captain monitors conditions in the days leading up to your trip. Tampa Bay's summer showers usually pass within an hour and often just shift the route; if conditions are genuinely unsafe, we work with you on rescheduling options. Safety calls are always the captain's.

How far in advance should I book my first charter?

2 to 4 weeks ahead is the sweet spot, and earlier for weekends and holidays. Last-minute availability is often possible on weekdays, so if the group decides on a Wednesday to go Friday, call (727) 609-2248 and we'll check the calendar.

What is included in the charter price?

Everything it takes to run your day: USCG-licensed captain, crew, and fuel at one all-inclusive rate of $500/hr weekdays and $600/hr weekends. Coolers, ice, life jackets in all sizes, the Bluetooth stereo, and Starlink WiFi are aboard. You bring the food and drinks.

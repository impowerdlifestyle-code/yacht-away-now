How to Plan the Perfect Yacht Proposal in St. Petersburg

Sunset timing, ring safety, crew coordination, and everything you need to pop the question on the water.

March 2026 Updated May 2026 • 12 min read
JW
By Captain Josh Wilson
Founder & USCG Master 100-Ton Captain

You have the ring and you have the person. What you need now is the moment. I have helped coordinate dozens of proposals aboard Yacht Away Now, and so far every one of them has gotten a yes, which I take no credit for. Here is how to plan one in St. Petersburg without leaving anything to chance.

Why It Works On the Water

Being on the water takes the noise out of it. There is no crowded restaurant with strangers watching, and no public park where somebody is filming you on their phone. On a private yacht it is the two of you and a crew that knows when to disappear and when to open the champagne.

Anchored off St. Petersburg with the sun going into the Gulf, nobody is watching and nothing is on a clock. No reservation is waiting on the table. That is the part people underestimate: the proposal can take as long as it takes.

St. Petersburg helps. The city faces west, so the sun goes down directly over open water every evening of the year. The barrier islands are out there, the Sunshine Skyway sits on the horizon, and dolphins come up alongside the boat often enough that I have stopped announcing them. Your partner usually feels the shift the moment we leave the dock.

The Best Time of Day To Ask

Golden hour, every time. The best proposals I have been part of happened in the 30 to 45 minutes before sunset, when the light goes warm and the sky starts turning over. The photographs are a bonus. The real reason is that everybody feels better in that light.

Sunset moves a lot across the year here. Rough guide for planning your departure:

  • Winter (December to February): sunset around 5:45 to 6:15 PM, so leave by 3:30 PM and you still get an unhurried cruise first.
  • Spring (March to May): sunset around 7:30 to 8:15 PM, so leave by 5:00 PM. This is peak proposal season, and the weather holds up better than any other stretch of the year.
  • Summer (June to August): sunset around 8:15 to 8:30 PM, so leave by 5:30 PM. The long days give you more water time either side of the moment.
  • Fall (September to November): sunset around 6:30 to 7:45 PM, so leave by 4:00 PM. Fewer boats out, and the light in that stretch is my favorite of the year.

Facing west across the Gulf, there is nothing between you and the horizon. No buildings, no trees, just water and color. The flybridge is where I would do it: elevated, private, 360 degrees of sunset. I will hold the yacht so the sun sits directly behind your partner when you ask.

Book the charter so the question lands about 20 minutes before actual sunset. That gives you the best light for photos and leaves you the rest of the sunset to watch together, engaged.

How to Keep the Ring Safe On a Boat

This is the question that keeps people up the night before, and fairly so. You are on a moving vessel surrounded by water, carrying the most expensive small object you have ever owned. Two ways to handle it.

Option 1: hand it to me at the marina. This is what most people do and what I recommend. Give me the box before your partner boards. We stow it in the cabin and bring it up at the right moment, or set it out with champagne and flowers on the flybridge while the two of you are down on the main deck. Nothing has ever gone wrong with this version.

Option 2: carry it yourself. If you want it on you the whole time, use a zippered pocket. Not an open pocket, not a jacket you might take off, and never a back pocket. Cargo shorts with a zip or a button are the right call. The test is whether the ring can work its way out when you sit down or lean over a rail.

The rule I will not bend on: the box never goes over the railing. Do not open it near the edge, and do not wave it at the horizon while you are talking. Propose in the middle of the flybridge or the cockpit, away from the rail. We have never lost a ring on this boat and I would like to keep it that way.

Should You Tell The Crew?

Yes, tell us. This is not a surprise you have to pull off on your own, and we have done it dozens of times. When you tell us in advance, here is what we can handle:

  • Music. Send us the song and we will have it queued on the Bluetooth stereo, ready to start on the cue you give us.
  • Champagne. A bottle chilled and standing by. The cork goes the second we hear a yes.
  • Photography timing. If you have a photographer aboard we get them in position before you kneel. If you do not, we will have phones out and stay well back.
  • Positioning. I put the yacht where the light works: sun behind your partner, skyline in frame, calm water underneath. That is the part I am actually useful for.
  • The setup. While you are both on the main deck, the crew can lay out rose petals, candles or a champagne display on the flybridge. You suggest going up to see the view, and it is already there.

The crew is discreet about it. The trick is making a heavily planned moment feel like it just happened, and they are good at that part.

Getting a Photographer Aboard Unnoticed

Yes, and I would. A few ways to get one aboard without tipping your partner off:

The friend approach. The photographer boards as one of your friends, dressed casually with a small bag. By the time the moment arrives they are already in position with the right lens. This works best when a few friends or family are aboard anyway.

The below-deck surprise. If the charter is just the two of you, the photographer waits in the cabin for the first part of the cruise. We signal them, they come up quietly, shoot the proposal from a distance, and then introduce themselves for the portraits afterward.

Drone. Some photographers will launch from the boat or from shore nearby. The shot is the yacht anchored in gold water with the two of you small on the flybridge, and it is the one people end up framing.

Crew photography. If a professional is not in the budget, we will shoot it on your phone. It will not match a photographer with a proper camera, but we know where to stand and what the light is doing, and you will come away with something worth keeping.

We work with several local St. Pete photographers who specialize in proposal and engagement shoots. Contact us and we can make introductions.

What Happens After She Says Yes

The question is the short part. Here is how the rest of a proposal charter usually goes:

The champagne. The crew pours and then leaves you alone on the flybridge for a few minutes. That gap is when it lands on people. The ring is on, the sun is going, and nobody needs anything from either of you.

The rest of the cruise. Most proposal charters run four to five hours, so you have two or three left afterward. Call your families, take more photos, open a bottle of wine. A lot of couples ask me to run past the St. Pete skyline after dark, and the city lights on the water are worth the detour.

Dinner. Book a table before you leave the dock. Nobody wants to be looking at a waitlist at nine o'clock with a new ring on. A few that work:

  • Cassis, a French-American bistro on Beach Drive. Quiet room, good wine list.
  • Sea Salt, upscale seafood, also on Beach Drive. Handsome room and a serious cocktail list.
  • The Birchwood Rooftop, a rooftop bar looking over the waterfront. More casual than the other two, and the right call if you want drinks and small plates rather than a full dinner.

All three are walkable or a short drive from the marina.

What a Yacht Proposal Costs

It costs less than most people assume. The numbers:

  • Standard charter: $500/hr on weekdays and $600/hr on weekends, all-inclusive with a 4-hour minimum. A proposal charter comes to $2,000 to $2,400 all in for the yacht, captain and fuel. The rate is the same whether there are 2 of you aboard or 13.
  • Sunset proposal package: if you want us handling the rose petals, the champagne setup on the flybridge, the music and the timing, the sunset packages run $2,000 to $2,500 depending on duration and add-ons.
  • Photography: an independent photographer runs $300 to $800 for a 2 to 3 hour proposal and engagement session. Drone work adds another $200 to $400.

Worth it? The average American proposal runs between $3,000 and $10,000 once you count the ring, the setting, dinner and the extras. A yacht proposal costs less than a restaurant buyout or a destination trip, and it buys you hours of private water time and a sunset nobody had to arrange.

You only ask this once. Put it somewhere you will both want to remember.

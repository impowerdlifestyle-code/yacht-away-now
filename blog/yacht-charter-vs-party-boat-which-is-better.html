Private Yacht Charter vs Party Boat: Which Is Right for You?

An honest comparison, including the cases where I would send you to a party boat.

March 2026 Updated May 2026 • 6 min read
JW
By Captain Josh Wilson
Founder & USCG Master 100-Ton Captain

You are planning a day or an evening on the water in Tampa Bay, and it comes down to two options: book a private yacht charter, or buy tickets on a shared party boat. Both get you out on the Gulf. That is where the similarity ends. Here is the honest version, including the situations where I tell people to book the party boat.

Who Else Is On the Boat

A party boat, sometimes called a booze cruise or a public charter, sells individual tickets. You share the vessel with dozens of strangers, and the operator sets the music, the schedule and the route. You get a spot on deck. The day belongs to the crowd.

A private yacht charter gives you the whole vessel. Your group, your guest list. Birthday, bachelorette, or just a quiet evening with friends, nobody else is competing for deck space, the aux cord or the good seat at the bow. For a lot of groups that settles it right there.

Customization and Flexibility

Party boats run fixed schedules on set routes. You board at a set time, cruise a set path, and come back when the operator says so. There is almost no room to change any of it.

On a private charter you have a say in the itinerary. Anchor at Shell Key for a swim, then run past the Skyway at sunset? Fine. Stay near downtown St. Pete and watch the skyline come on? Also fine. At Yacht Away Now we build the route around what the group actually wants instead of running the same loop every time.

Luxury and Comfort

Party boats are designed around capacity. Bench seating, not much shade, one bar, and a head that sees a lot of traffic. They work. Comfort is not what they optimize for.

A private yacht is a different category. Our 52ft Marquis Flybridge has three full decks, a shaded flybridge, a salon, a full galley kitchen and proper seating throughout, all for a maximum of 13 guests. Nobody is packed in.

Group Size and Who It Suits

Party boats suit solo travelers, couples and small groups who want something casual without committing to a whole vessel. If meeting people is part of the appeal and a loud crowd does not bother you, it is a cheap and genuinely fun afternoon.

Private charters suit groups of 4 to 13 who already know each other. Birthdays, proposals, family reunions, corporate outings, bachelorette parties. Having the whole boat is what makes those days feel like an occasion rather than an outing.

Pricing and Value

Party boats look cheaper because you buy tickets individually, usually $30 to $75 a head. That price covers a short cruise with limited amenities, and food and drinks are extra at bar prices.

A private charter is a flat rate for the whole boat. Split across 8 to 13 people, the per-person number lands closer to party boat territory than most people expect. Add BYOB food and drinks with no markup and the gap narrows again.

Experience Quality

This is where the gap is widest. A party boat gives you a boat ride. On a private charter you get a captain who knows where to go, the freedom to set the pace, music you chose, food you chose, and three decks to move around without brushing past a stranger every time you stand up.

Then there is the part that does not fit in a table. Pulling off the dock with your own people aboard, drinks cold, the St. Petersburg skyline going small behind you. I have watched that land on a lot of faces, and it does not happen on a shared vessel.

The Real Cost Math for a Group of 10

The "party boats are cheaper" instinct falls apart once you run the per-person math at a normal bachelorette or birthday headcount. Here is a realistic comparison for a Saturday in May with a group of 10:

Party boat. 10 tickets at $65 each on a Tampa Bay sunset cruise is $650 to board. Three drinks each at the cash bar averages $35 a head, so $350. Tip the staff 18% and add $180. Total around $1,180 for 2 hours, no privacy, and crowded photos.

Private yacht. Our weekend rate is $2,400 all in for 4 hours, captain, crew and fuel included. BYO food and drinks from Publix or Trader Tilly's runs about $300 for snacks and bar for 10. Optional crew gratuity at 10 to 15% is $240 to $360. Total around $2,700 for 4 hours, with the boat to yourselves.

Per hour that is $590 for the party boat and $675 for the yacht. Per person per hour it is $59 against $67. Eight dollars an hour each is what separates a ticket on a crowded deck from a private vessel with a custom route, anchored swim stops, no bar markup and a captain working only for your group. The cheaper framing tends to leave that part out.

Tampa Bay Party Boats Worth Knowing About

To be fair to the category, they are not all the same and a few are very good at what they do. If a party boat is genuinely the right fit for your group, these are the ones I point people toward:

  • StarLite Cruises, a dinner cruise out of John's Pass. Two decks, full bar, hot buffet, narrated sightseeing. Good for couples who want a sit-down meal with a Gulf view and no planning. Around $70-$90 per person.
  • Tropical Cruises, a midday run out of Hubbard's Marina. Calm-water bay tour, snacks available, kids welcome. Around $40-$55 per person.
  • Captain Memo's Pirate Cruise, a themed cruise out of Clearwater Beach with a costumed crew. It sits somewhere between a boat trip and a stage show, and it works for a family reunion with a wide age spread. Around $45 per adult, $35 per kid.
  • Calypso Queen, lunch and dinner buffet cruises out of Pier 22 in Bradenton. Bigger boat, more formal, popular with retirement-age groups. Around $60-$85 per person.

None of them compete with a private charter on privacy or customization, and none of them are trying to. They are hospitality operations with food and music, and they do that well. The private yacht wins when the point of the day is that it revolves around your group.

When a Yacht Is The Wrong Answer

Four situations where the honest answer is not a yacht charter:

  • Two people, no occasion. A $2,000 charter is hard to justify for a casual day out. A dinner cruise or sunset party boat at $50-$80 a ticket is the right call.
  • Deciding this morning. Charters usually need 24 to 48 hours notice, and 1 to 2 weeks for a weekend date. Party boats sell tickets right at the dock, so a last-minute plan leaves you one option.
  • Guests with limited mobility. Most Tampa Bay party boats are fully accessible, with ADA-compliant ramps and accessible heads. Our yacht has stairs to the flybridge and to the cabins below, so a larger commercial vessel may be the better boat.
  • Two hours of dancing and nothing else. If nobody wants an anchor stop, a narrated route or photos, a party boat with a DJ already does that. You would be paying us for flexibility you are not going to use.

We send roughly 5 to 8 percent of inquiries each year to party-boat operators because their situation fits better there. I would rather they booked the right boat than overpaid for mine.

So, Which Should You Choose?

If you want a quick, cheap outing and you enjoy a party atmosphere with strangers, book the party boat. That is a real answer, not a consolation prize.

If you are planning a celebration or a milestone, or you just want a day built around your own group, the private charter earns the difference. Privacy, comfort and flexibility all come from the same fact: the boat is yours for the afternoon.

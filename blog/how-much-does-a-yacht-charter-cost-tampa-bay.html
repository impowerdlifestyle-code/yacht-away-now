How Much Does a Yacht Charter Cost in Tampa Bay? 2026 Pricing Guide

Real numbers, no hidden fees — what a private yacht charter actually costs on Florida's Gulf Coast.

July 2026 7 min read
JW
By Captain Josh Wilson
Founder & USCG Master 100-Ton Captain

Search "yacht charter Tampa Bay" and you'll find plenty of stunning photos — and almost no prices. Most companies make you call, email, and negotiate before you learn what a day on the water actually costs. We do it differently. Here is exactly what a private yacht charter costs in Tampa Bay in 2026, using the same transparent pricing we publish on our pricing page.

The Short Answer

A private charter aboard our 52ft Marquis Flybridge costs $500 per hour Monday through Thursday and $600 per hour Friday through Sunday, with a 4-hour minimum. Both rates are all-inclusive: your USCG-licensed captain, crew, fuel, coolers, ice, and cleaning are all covered. There is nothing added at checkout.

That puts the most common trips at:

4-hour weekday charter — $2,000. The best value on the calendar. Same yacht, same crew, same sandbars, calmer waterways.
4-hour weekend charter — $2,400. The classic Saturday afternoon on Tampa Bay.
5-hour weekend sunset cruise — $3,000. Our most-booked trip: depart mid-afternoon, swim at a sandbar, and watch the sun drop behind the Gulf on the ride home.
Multi-day Bahamas and Florida Keys trips — from $9,500. Covered in detail in our Bahamas by yacht guide.

What "All-Inclusive" Actually Covers

This is where Tampa Bay charter pricing gets misleading. Many operators advertise a low base rate, then add fuel surcharges, captain fees, per-person charges, docking fees, and cleaning fees once you're ready to book. A "cheap" charter can grow 30–50% between the first quote and the final invoice.

Our rate includes the captain and crew, all fuel (even for sandbar runs and island stops), coolers and ice for your BYOB drinks, use of the three decks, premium stereo, Starlink WiFi, and the full galley kitchen. The number you see is the number you pay.

Cost Per Person: The Math That Surprises People

A yacht charter sounds like a luxury reserved for special occasions — and it is a luxury — but split across a group, the per-person cost is closer to a night out than most people expect. Our Marquis hosts up to 13 guests, so a $2,400 weekend charter is about $185 per person, and a $2,000 weekday trip drops to roughly $154 per person. Compare that to a group dinner on Beach Drive with drinks, and four hours on a private yacht — swimming, dolphin spotting, sunset from the flybridge — starts looking like the better deal.

Weekday vs Weekend: When to Book

If your dates are flexible, book Monday through Thursday. You save $100 per hour, the waterways around Shell Key and Egmont Key are noticeably quieter, and sandbar anchorages that fill up on Saturdays are nearly private. Read more about seasonal timing in our month-by-month weather guide.

Pricing the Longer Days

The 4-hour minimum covers most trips, but the hourly structure makes extending simple. A 5-hour weekday charter is $2,500 and a weekend version is $3,000, which is the sweet spot for a sandbar swim plus a full sunset finish. A 6-hour day runs $3,000 weekday or $3,600 weekend, and that's the block we recommend for the full two-island run to Egmont Key and Shell Key, covered stop by stop in our island day trip itinerary. Because every hour is all-inclusive, adding time never triggers fuel surcharges or add-on fees. The sixth hour costs exactly what the first one did.

Per-Person Math at Every Group Size

The per-person number moves fast with headcount, and it's worth running before you decide the guest list. On a 4-hour weekday charter at $2,000 total:

13 guests: about $154 each. The full-boat rate, and the best per-person value on the calendar.
10 guests: $200 each. Still comfortably in group-dinner territory.
8 guests: $250 each. A typical birthday or bachelorette crew.
6 guests: about $334 each. Room to spare across all three decks.

On a $2,400 weekend charter the same splits land at roughly $185, $240, $300, and $400. The practical takeaway: a group that fills more seats pays dramatically less per head for the identical trip, so before you book, ask the two friends who were on the fence.

What About Gratuity?

One line item is not baked into the rate, on purpose: gratuity. It is entirely at your discretion and never added automatically at checkout. If the crew earns it, 10 to 15 percent of the base charter price is customary, which works out to $200 to $300 on a $2,000 weekday trip. It goes to the captain and crew who ran your day, and the choice stays yours.

How to Keep the Total Down

A few honest levers, from the people setting the prices. Book Monday through Thursday and save $100 per hour before you've optimized anything else. Fill the boat, since the rate is per hour rather than per person and empty seats are the most expensive thing aboard. Use the BYOB policy: there is no drink minimum and no marked-up bar, so your cooler costs what the store charged you (just no glass bottles). And book two to four weeks ahead for weekend dates, not to save money directly, but because the cheapest charter is the one on the date you actually wanted instead of a compromise slot.

Our Second Yacht: Sly Fox

For smaller groups and overnight trips, our 3-stateroom yacht Sly Fox runs $650 per hour on weekdays and $750 per hour on weekends, with multi-day Gulf Coast itineraries from $5,000 and an optional private chef. She hosts up to 6 guests and is built for couples' trips, anniversaries, and multi-day cruising.

Booking and Deposits

A 50% deposit locks in your date, with the balance due seven days before departure. If your captain calls off a charter for weather or unsafe conditions, you reschedule at no additional cost and your booking stays valid for twelve months. Weekend sunset slots book out first — in spring and summer, the best Saturday evenings are often gone two to three weeks ahead. How Much Does a Yacht Charter Cost — FAQ

How much does it cost to rent a yacht in Tampa Bay?

A private 52ft yacht charter with Yacht Away Now costs $500 per hour on weekdays (Mon–Thu) and $600 per hour on weekends (Fri–Sun), all-inclusive with a 4-hour minimum. That means a 4-hour weekday charter is $2,000 total — captain, crew, and fuel included.

What does all-inclusive mean on a yacht charter?

All-inclusive means the hourly rate covers the USCG-licensed captain, crew, fuel, coolers, ice, water toys, and cleaning. There are no fuel surcharges, per-person fees, or mandatory gratuity lines added at checkout. You bring food and drinks (charters are BYOB) — everything else is covered.

How much does a yacht charter cost per person?

With up to 13 guests aboard, a $2,400 weekend charter works out to roughly $185 per person — comparable to a nice dinner out, for four hours on a private 52ft yacht with captain and crew.

Is a deposit required to book a yacht charter?

Yes — a 50% deposit secures your date and time, with the balance due seven days before departure. Booking early matters most for weekend sunset slots, which sell out first in spring and summer.

How much does a 6-hour yacht charter cost in Tampa Bay?

A 6-hour charter aboard the 52ft Marquis is $3,000 on weekdays (Mon–Thu) and $3,600 on weekends (Fri–Sun), all-inclusive. That's the block we recommend for the full Egmont Key and Shell Key two-island day.

Is gratuity included in the yacht charter price?

No. Gratuity is entirely at your discretion and is never added at checkout. Customary is 10 to 15 percent of the base charter price, which is $200 to $300 on a $2,000 weekday trip, and it goes to the captain and crew.
